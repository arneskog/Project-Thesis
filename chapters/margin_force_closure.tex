\section{Requirements}
\label{sec:margin-requirements}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Existence and uniqueness}: every configuration gets exactly one well-defined value.
    \item \textbf{Metric-like}: it works like a score, so configurations can be compared and ranked.
    \item \textbf{Continuous and smooth}: needed for optimization and path planning.
    \item \textbf{Negative, zero, positive}: $M<0$ not held, $M=0$ on the edge, $M>0$ frictional form closure and potentially form closure.
    \item \textbf{Prefer form closure, accept frictional form closure}: Frictional from closure only configurations score $d^*_\mu$, form closure adds $\kappa*d^*_0$ on top, continuously.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{LP2 optimal value as a margin.}
The optimal value $d^\star$ of the frictional form-closure
LP~\eqref{eq:lp2} can be taken as a \emph{force closure margin}:
\begin{equation}
d^\star
\begin{cases}
= 0, & \text{no frictional force closure } (\mathbf{0} \notin \operatorname{int}(\mathcal{W})), \\
> 0, & \text{frictional force closure holds } (\mathbf{0} \in \operatorname{int}(\mathcal{W})),
\end{cases}
\label{eq:fc-margin-interpretation}
\end{equation}
with larger $d^\star$ indicating that the origin sits further inside the
wrench space $\mathcal{W}$~\eqref{eq:wrench-space} --- i.e.\ a more robust
closure, better able to withstand disturbances or produce the propulsive
wrench required by the locomotion gait. This margin serves as the continuous
force-closure metric used in the path planner: candidate configurations are
scored (or filtered) by $d^\star$, computed once per contact set by solving
the linear program~\eqref{eq:lp2}.
